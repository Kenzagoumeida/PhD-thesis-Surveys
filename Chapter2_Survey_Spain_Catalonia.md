Livestock farming is claimed to have a significant contribution to emissions of nitrates, ammonia (NH3), and greenhouse gases such as methane (CH4) and nitrous oxide (N2O), affecting air, water and soil quality (warming, acidification, eutrophication). The three main emission sources in animal production are enteric fermentation, manure and feed production, with feed being a major contributor of EU farm emissions.

Soybean (protein source), mostly imported from the Americas, has been widely questioned for its environmental impact, largely due to Land-Use Change in the areas of origin over the last 20 years.

Optimising feed formulation is crucial to reducing farm emissions: the amount of soybean-meal protein can be reduced by adding commercial crystalline amino acids — an interesting strategy from an environmental perspective.

Catalonia has a strong interest in improving the environmental performance of pig production given the sector's importance in the region. A low crude protein nutritional strategy could significantly reduce CO2, CH4 and NH3 emissions and the amount of soybean imported from outside the EU. A well-balanced low-protein diet (in amino acids) could also improve pig welfare by reducing diarrhoea and antibiotic use.

However, low-protein diets cost 1.84% (€242.15/t feed) to 7.80% (€256.32/t feed) more than the normal diet (€237.77/t feed). Production cost of 1 kg Live Weight (LW) rises from €1.01 (normal diet) to €1.04 (very low crude protein diet), which would negatively affect producers' incomes.

One way to motivate producers to adopt this feeding alternative would be to compensate them for the ecosystem services generated. The following section assesses your willingness to pay for these ecosystem services.
